% Abstract
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Lung diseases such as tuberculosis (TB), COVID-19 and pneumonia are major causes of illness and death, and in many regions there are not enough radiologists to read chest X-rays quickly. Many student and demo systems train a separate model for each disease, so the models cannot be compared fairly and one image cannot get a single answer covering all diseases. This report presents an \textbf{AI Agent for Lung Disease Detection} that sorts chest X-rays into four classes: Normal, Tuberculosis, COVID-19 and Pneumonia. Three models, EfficientNetB3 and DenseNet121 (ImageNet transfer learning) and a custom CNN (trained from scratch), are trained on one shared dataset built from three public sources. Duplicate images are removed, the data is split by patient, class weights handle the small classes, and the transfer models share the same head and fine-tune about 43\% of their weights, so the comparison is fair.

A Flask web app checks that each upload is a chest X-ray, runs all three models, and shows each model's probabilities together with a combined soft-voting verdict. On the held-out test set ($n=482$), DenseNet121 was the best single model with 90.87\% accuracy and 89.14\% balanced accuracy, while the ensemble reached 92.32\% accuracy and 93.07\% balanced accuracy. COVID-19 had high recall but limited precision because it is the smallest class, and a source-leakage probe (88.17\% against a 49.79\% baseline) showed a moderate source signal. The system is a research demo built on public data and is \textbf{not} a clinical diagnostic device.
